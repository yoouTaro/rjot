Keep the text you highlight.

Three moves, no switching.

  1. 01

    Select

    Highlight text anywhere: a web page, an editor, a reply from an AI.

  2. 02

    ⇧ ⇧

    Tap Shift twice. A short pop tells you it worked.

  3. 03

    Kept

    It's a note in rjot, with the app it came from and the time.

Small, on purpose.

A to-do list, a clipboard and a scratchpad. That's all it tries to be.

A queue for prompts

Write prompts for later. Copy one into ChatGPT, Claude or Cursor and it's checked off.

Color labels

Tag a note with one of six colors, then filter the list to one color.

Sensitive mode

Note text is blurred until you hold ⌥, and the window stays out of screenshots and screen sharing.

Quick note

Nothing selected? ⇧ ⇧ opens the note box. Type, press ⌘ ↩, and rjot gets out of the way.

Six themes

Each in dark and light, following your Mac or set by hand.

A scratchpad

One page for anything else, saved as you type.

Nothing leaves your Mac.

rjot has no account, no sync and no analytics. It never connects to the network.

  • One file you can read. Your notes are plain JSON in ~/Library/Application Support/dev.rjot.rjot.
  • Passwords are skipped. If a password manager marks a copy as concealed, rjot doesn't save it.
  • Your clipboard comes back. When rjot has to copy to read a selection, it puts your clipboard back the way it was.
  • Open source. Every line is on GitHub, under the MIT license.

Questions

Why does it need Accessibility access?

Accessibility access lets rjot hear the two Shift taps and read the text you selected in another app. macOS asks once. Turn rjot on under System Settings, Privacy & Security, Accessibility.

macOS says it can't open rjot.

rjot isn't notarized by Apple yet. The first time, right-click rjot.app and choose Open, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine rjot.app.

Does it work in browsers and Electron apps?

Yes. Most native apps tell rjot what's selected. For the ones that don't, rjot presses ⌘ C, reads the text, and puts your clipboard back the way it was.

How is it different from a clipboard manager?

A clipboard manager keeps everything you copy. rjot keeps only what you ask it to, and leaves your clipboard as it was.
